\subsection{Nine agents are worth fewer than two}

In every system, most items were answered correctly by all nine agents or by none, a U-shaped distribution that the beta-binomial with the observed $\phi$ reproduces and the binomial for independent agents does not (\cref{fig:hist}).
The Independent MAS provided $N_{\mathrm{eff}}=1.17$ to $1.39$ for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash and Claude Haiku 4.5, and $1.46$ to $1.99$ for Qwen 3.8 Flash (\cref{fig:panels}).
For GPT-5 mini, Gemini and Claude, Independent and Decentralized multi-agent gains stayed within $-3.2$ to 5.6 percentage points (pp) (\cref{fig:panels}a).
Qwen's gains arose before the vote: mean agent minus single-agent accuracy was 3.1 to 10.3~pp, while majority minus mean agent accuracy was 1.1 to 3.3~pp (\cref{fig:panels}b).
Observed majority accuracy differed from the beta-binomial prediction by $-0.07$~pp on average (SD 0.84~pp) across 108 yes/no systems (\cref{fig:teaser}).
Debate drove final-answer $\phi$ to 0.995 to 1.000 for GPT-5 mini, Gemini and Claude (\cref{fig:panels}c; \cref{tab:s-mas}).

\subsection{Shared wrong answers follow the language prior}
On MedXpertQA-MM, 64.2\% to 76.2\% of shared wrong answers matched the blank-image majority (\cref{fig:prior}d; \cref{tab:s-prior}).
Blank images cost single agents 11.2 to 40.0~pp on VQA-RAD and SLAKE, versus 2.0 to 4.0~pp for GPT-5 mini, Claude and Qwen on MedXpertQA-MM and 18.0~pp for Gemini (\cref{fig:prior}a).
With blank images, Gemini, Claude and Qwen reached $\phi=0.831$ to $0.982$ on VQA-RAD and SLAKE, up from 0.439 to 0.785 with original images (\cref{fig:prior}b; \cref{tab:s-resolution}).

\subsection{Models differ in where they look}
Gemini's occlusion sensitivity was 32.9~pp, versus 17.1~pp for GPT-5 mini and 3.2~pp for Claude (\cref{fig:region}a).
Pair accuracy was 74.0\% for Gemini, 59.0\% for GPT-5 mini and 38.5\% for Claude (\cref{fig:region}b; \cref{tab:s-region}).
Occluding the liver changed every Gemini agent's answer and no Claude agent's answer; Claude answered yes to both paired images (\cref{fig:region-example}).

\subsection{Perception sets accuracy; a shared report sets correlation}
Gemini reports raised the other reasoning modules' accuracy by 6.1 to 16.3~pp over reading the image (\cref{fig:report}).
With identical Gemini reports on MedXpertQA-MM, reasoning modules reached 74.5\% (Gemini), 62.0\% (GPT-5 mini) and 45.6\% (Claude).
Sharing one report raised $\phi$ to 0.800 to 0.990, from 0.641 to 0.818 with own reports from the same model (\cref{fig:report}b; \cref{tab:s-report}).
Only Gemini's report mentioned the dilated bile ducts in \cref{fig:report-example}; every reasoning module answered correctly from it and none from the other reports.

\subsection{Changing the model decorrelates errors most}
Self-consistency agents of one model already make correlated errors, $\phi$ 0.692 to 0.915 for GPT-5 mini, Gemini and Claude and 0.442 to 0.629 for Qwen (\cref{tab:s-contrasts}).
Changing the model lowered $\phi$ by 0.470 to 0.492, the largest reduction among tested contrasts (\cref{fig:contrasts}).
Changing the role produced the smallest reduction, 0.033 to 0.060; contrast adjustment, in-context examples, report and perception module lay between.
On MedXpertQA-MM, changing the reasoning module under an identical report lowered $\phi$ by 0.430, more than changing the perception module (0.263; \cref{tab:s-contrasts}).

\subsection{Debate removes correct minority answers}
Debate changed 41.5\% to 100.0\% of correct initial minority answers to wrong answers, versus 1.0\% to 6.5\% of correct majority answers (\cref{fig:conformity}).
On MedXpertQA-MM, conformity was 7.2\% to 23.6\% for correct answers that differed from the same agent's blank-image answer, versus 2.6\% to 6.3\% for those that matched (\cref{tab:s-conformity}).
