\paragraph{Multi-agent systems in medicine.}
Role-prompted medical MAS improve medical question answering~\cite{kim2024mdagents,tang2024medagents}, and their gains shrink as the base model becomes more capable~\cite{kim2026scaling}. Multiagent debate supports revision from peer responses~\cite{du2024debate}; benchmarks and debate analyses characterize collaboration and diversity~\cite{medagentboard,demystifying_mad}. We connect these systems' radiology accuracy to their effective number of independent agents and identify shared components behind correlated errors.

\paragraph{Correlated errors and aggregation.}
Dependence limits information pooling~\cite{clemen1985limits}, and correlated wrong answers occur across language models~\cite{correlated2025}. Kish's design effect relates correlation to effective sample size~\cite{kish1965survey}; diagnostic pooling benefits from independent judgments with similar accuracy~\cite{kurvers2016pooling}. We predict majority accuracy from mean agent accuracy and error correlation, then measure changes in dependence through matched component contrasts.

\paragraph{Language priors and visual reliance in VLMs.}
Visual-question-answering benchmarks show that language priors affect answers~\cite{goyal2017vqa,chen2024mmstar} and that models can fail on paired images requiring different answers~\cite{li2024naturalbench,tong2024eyes}. Medical probes expose diagnosis failures and flawed image comprehension behind correct answers~\cite{yan2025worse,jin2024hidden}. Counterfactual grounding tests visual reliance~\cite{zafar2026see}, while Prism separates perception from reasoning through visual descriptions~\cite{qiao2024prism}. We trace shared MAS errors through blank-image answers and report swaps that isolate dependence on common findings.
