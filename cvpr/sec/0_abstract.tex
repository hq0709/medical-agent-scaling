\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent systems built from one vision-language model (VLM) seek complementary judgments from agents that share the same weights. We evaluate four proprietary and five open-weight VLMs on VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and MedXpertQA-MM. We compare a single agent and self-consistency (nine samples, majority vote) with Independent and Decentralized multi-agent systems (MAS) of nine agents. Controlled image manipulations and shared reports probe the language prior and separate perception from reasoning. The Independent MAS provides $1.17$ to $1.99$ effective independent agents ($N_{\mathrm{eff}}$). A beta-binomial model predicts majority accuracy from mean agent accuracy and error correlation across all yes/no systems; prediction errors have SD 0.84 percentage points (pp). On MedXpertQA-MM, $64.2\%$ to $76.2\%$ of shared wrong answers (at least seven of nine agents wrong) match blank-image (uniform gray) majorities. Sharing a query-agnostic report raises error correlation to $0.800$--$0.990$ relative to own reports. Changing the model lowers error correlation by $0.470$--$0.492$, compared with $0.033$--$0.060$ for roles. During debate, agents change $41.5\%$--$100.0\%$ of correct initial minority answers to wrong answers. A second reading lowers error correlation most when it comes from a different model.
\end{abstract}
