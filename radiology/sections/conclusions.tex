\section{Conclusions}

Reading panels built from the same vision-language model shared errors that limited the benefit of majority voting. Panel accuracy depended on both individual reader accuracy and error correlation. Question-text priors contributed to shared errors, and a shared findings report increased error correlation. Different models reduced error correlation more than changes in roles, image rendering, or reference cases within a model. Discussion increased agreement and overturned correct minority answers, including answers dependent on image evidence. These findings concern closed radiology questions without human readers. Evaluating reading panels and second reads in radiology requires measuring individual accuracy, use of image evidence, and errors corrected or introduced by another reader.
