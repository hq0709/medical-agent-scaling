\section{Discussion}

A reading panel of nine readers built from one VLM did not provide nine independent reads. Shared errors limited the benefit of majority voting, and discussion increased agreement without consistently improving accuracy. Differences in the findings available to readers and in the models processing them reduced error correlation more than changes in roles. The same error correlation and rise during discussion occurred for text-only clinical questions, from 0.758 to 0.990 (Supplementary S5). Shared errors therefore extend beyond image interpretation to the way readers built from the same model answer clinical questions.

The panel advantage depended on the accuracy of its readers and their error correlation. Qwen 3.8 Flash had less correlated errors than the other models and gained more over the single reader. On VQA-RAD, its mean panel reader exceeded the single reader by 10.3 percentage points, and aggregation added 3.3 percentage points. Across the yes/no panels, observed majority accuracy minus the beta-binomial prediction averaged $-$0.07 percentage points (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}). This agreement connects the limited return from additional readers to measured error correlation. Counting responses as independent observations overstates the information in a panel when the same items elicit errors from most readers.

Discussion preserved correct majority answers more often than correct minority answers. On MedXpertQA-MM, every model abandoned correct answers that differed from its gray-image answers more often than correct answers that matched them (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}). Correct answers dependent on image evidence were also more often in the initial minority on that set. Discussion thus exposed those answers to a majority sharing an incorrect question-text prior. After discussion, 8.9\% to 70.0\% of unanimous answers were wrong. Radiologists' deference to incorrect AI BI-RADS suggestions provides a clinical example of accepting another reader's assessment despite its error.\cite{dratsch2023automation} In these panels, the final consensus reflects both the initial readings and subsequent deference to peers.

The gray-image experiments connected shared errors to the question-text prior. Removing the image lowered accuracy toward chance on VQA-RAD and SLAKE and increased error correlation for Gemini, Claude Haiku, and Qwen (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence}). On MedXpertQA-MM, most consensus errors repeated the gray-image majority, ranging from 64.2\% to 76.2\% across models (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}). Readers therefore often reached the same wrong answer with and without image evidence. Language priors in visual question answering and poor performance under medical probing provide related evidence.\cite{goyal2017vqa,yan2025worse} Clinical information also changes diagnostic test interpretation.\cite{loy2004clinical} Here, question text supplied information that steered readers toward an answer even when the image required a different one.

Image interventions distinguished use of the relevant anatomy from answering correctly through the question text. Gemini had the greatest panel-reader region dependence on SLAKE-target, at 32.9 percentage points, compared with 3.2 percentage points for Claude Haiku (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}). Target occlusion changed answers more than matched control occlusion, directly connecting responses to the queried region. The paired-image experiment held the question constant and required opposite answers for different images. Readers frequently gave the same answer to both. Together, these interventions explain why accuracy on isolated questions is insufficient evidence of image use, consistent with correct medical answers accompanied by flawed image comprehension.\cite{jin2024hidden}

Seeing and judging each contributed to report-pathway accuracy (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}). Gemini reports improved GPT-5 mini and Claude Haiku on every data set relative to reading images directly. Gemini as reasoner performed worse with its own reports on MedXpertQA-MM than with images. Given identical Gemini reports on that set, Gemini achieved 74.5\% accuracy, GPT-5 mini achieved 62.0\%, and Claude Haiku achieved 45.6\%. The full comparison therefore identifies both the perceiver's extraction of findings and the reasoner's selection of an answer as sources of performance differences. Introducing an intermediate report did not itself improve reading; the findings supplied and the model interpreting them determined the result.

Sharing a report made readers' errors more alike. Across reasoners and data sets, shared-report error correlation ranged from 0.800 to 0.990 and exceeded correlation with separate reports from the same model. Fixing the findings removed much of the variation between readers, locating an important source of their differences in seeing. Reports also omitted queried SLAKE targets among both correct and incorrect answers. A question-blind report makes the extracted findings available for inspection, while its omissions determine which image information reaches judging. Repeated judgments from a shared report consequently depend on the same account of the image.

The matched contrasts formed a graded ladder of reductions in error correlation (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}). On VQA-RAD, mean changes over models were $-$0.048 for role, $-$0.092 for rendering, and $-$0.110 for reference cases. Roles changed instructions; renderings changed the presentation of the same image; private reference cases changed the examples available to each reader. These interventions progressively changed more of the information used in a reading while retaining the underlying model.

Continuing the VQA-RAD ladder, the mean changes were $-$0.199 for report, $-$0.311 for perceiver, and $-$0.470 for model. Separate reports supplied different findings, different perceivers changed the models extracting those findings, and different models changed both seeing and judging. The ordering also held on SLAKE and, for the evaluated contrasts, MedXpertQA-MM. None of the within-model changes brought error correlation near the level of readers from different models. Changing reasoners while holding the report fixed also reduced error correlation, with the greatest reduction on MedXpertQA-MM.

Complementary errors and adequate reader accuracy together determine the value of a second read. Private reference cases lowered error correlation across the evaluated models and formats, while panel accuracy varied (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}). Claude Haiku's SLAKE panel accuracy with text references remained 78.0\% under both shared and private assignment. Gemini's private text references increased panel accuracy on SLAKE. Human evidence likewise supports pooling independent judgments of similar accuracy,\cite{kurvers2016pooling} and double reading increased mammographic cancer detection.\cite{taylor2008double} For AI reading panels, the evidence supports evaluating a second reader by the errors it corrects and introduces, alongside its individual accuracy and correlation with the first reader.

This study used closed questions from public data sets and included no human readers or clinical reporting. Each condition used a single sampling seed; item bootstrap intervals did not measure variation across seeds. API models were evaluated through a single router at one time point. Gray-image abstentions counted as wrong; correlation among answered items was included as a sensitivity analysis.
