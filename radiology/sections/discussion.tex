\section{Discussion}

Correlated errors limited the contribution of additional readers built from one VLM (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}b). These measurements extend the finding of correlated errors across language models to reading panels answering questions about radiology images.\cite{correlated2025} In a companion analysis of text-only clinical questions, error correlation was of the same magnitude as the imaging values and rose during discussion (unpublished data; Supplementary Section S5).

The beta-binomial fit explains the observed majority-vote accuracy on yes/no items in terms of mean reader accuracy and error correlation (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}d,e). High error correlation limits the contribution of majority voting because readers tend to be wrong on the same items. Qwen 3.8 Flash's larger gain over the single reader came mainly from more accurate panel readers; the aggregation gain was smaller (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}a,e).

Conformity during discussion reduced the contribution of correct minority answers to the final consensus (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}). Readers changed correct initial answers to a wrong answer when they revised their responses after seeing peer answers. This pattern is consistent with conformity in multi-agent debate.\cite{demystifying_mad} Automation bias in mammography is a clinical parallel: radiologists accepted incorrect AI suggestions for Breast Imaging Reporting and Data System categories.\cite{dratsch2023automation}

The language prior contributed to correlated errors on MedXpertQA-MM, where shared wrong answers often matched blank-image majorities (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}). Agreement with the blank-image answer identifies decisions that persisted when visual findings were unavailable. The influence of question text parallels the effect of clinical information on diagnostic test interpretation in Loy and Irwig's systematic review.\cite{loy2004clinical}

Occlusion sensitivity identified differences among models in their dependence on the queried anatomy (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}a,b). The comparison with an equal-sized control region distinguishes sensitivity to the queried anatomy from sensitivity to occlusion elsewhere.\cite{zeiler2014visualizing} Gemini 3.7 Flash's stronger occlusion sensitivity indicates greater dependence on the queried anatomy than in the other proprietary models. Single readers' answers differed between paired images in 42.5\% to 75.0\% of pairs, although every pair had opposite reference answers (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}c). Blank-image accuracy losses and occlusion sensitivity in the open-weight probes show that these effects also occurred without sampling (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}b; Supplementary Fig.~S1a).

Perception and interpretation each contributed to accuracy in the report conditions (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}a). Separating these stages, as in Prism, allowed the same findings to be evaluated by different interpretation models.\cite{qiao2024prism} Gemini 3.7 Flash reports improved the other interpretation models' accuracy, while accuracy differed among models reading identical reports. Higher error correlation in the shared-report condition than in the own-report condition identifies common findings as a source of correlated errors (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}b). A query-agnostic findings report makes the extracted findings available for inspection; its omissions restrict the visual information available to the interpretation model.

Changing the model reduced error correlation most, which supports using a different model for a second reading (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}b). Changing the perception model ranked next on VQA-RAD and SLAKE. On MedXpertQA-MM, changing the interpretation model reduced error correlation more than changing the perception model. On VQA-RAD and SLAKE, within-model reductions were ordered from smallest to largest as roles, contrast adjustments, reference cases, and reports. The comparison of shared and private cases supports assessing a second reading by the errors it corrects and introduces (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}). For Claude Haiku 4.5, lower error correlation accompanied equal or lower panel accuracy.

This evaluation concerned closed questions from public data sets. Its scope did not include human readers or clinical reporting.
