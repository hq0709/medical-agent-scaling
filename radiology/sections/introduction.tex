\section{Introduction}

Double reading of screening mammograms increased cancer detection and recall rates compared with single reading.\cite{taylor2008double} In diagnostic radiology, a systematic review reported discrepancy rates of 0.4\% to 22\%, depending on the setting.\cite{geijer2018double} Radiology distinguishes perceptual and interpretive errors.\cite{bruno2015error} Here, perception is the extraction of visual findings; interpretation is the selection of an answer from the findings and medical knowledge. Pooling diagnostic judgments outperformed the best doctor when doctors had similar accuracy.\cite{kurvers2016pooling} The value of another reader depends on both individual accuracy and error correlation. Artificial intelligence (AI) systems have also been evaluated as second readers, including a simulation of double reading in breast cancer screening.\cite{mckinney2020breast}

Multi-agent systems built from large language models emulate a clinical consultation. Published medical systems assigned clinical roles to several instances of a model, which answered separately before discussing their answers or voting.\cite{kim2024mdagents,tang2024medagents} A reading panel applies this arrangement to questions about radiology images. An evaluation of agent systems found that the benefit of collaboration shrank as the underlying model became more capable.\cite{kim2026scaling} Another evaluation found correlated errors across language models, including models with different architectures and providers.\cite{correlated2025} In a reading panel built from one vision-language model (VLM), every reader shares the model's weights. These include the vision encoder and the language model. Sampling and role prompts change the text of each reader's response while the model and visual evidence remain shared.

Models exploited language priors, or answer information in the question and options alone; accuracy decreased when similar images required different answers to the same question.\cite{goyal2017vqa} In medical visual question answering, leading multimodal models performed worse than random guessing on simple probing questions about diagnosis.\cite{yan2025worse} Analysis of medical image questions also found correct answers accompanied by flawed image comprehension.\cite{jin2024hidden} Replacing an image with a blank image (a uniform gray image of the same size) lowered medical VLM accuracy by at most 6.5 percentage points.\cite{zafar2026see} A radiology data set constructed to remove such shortcuts reduced text-only accuracy to near chance.\cite{butsanets2026radimagenetvqa}

The aim of this study was to measure error correlation among VLM readers answering radiology questions and identify its sources. We evaluated four proprietary VLMs and five open-weight VLMs on three data sets. These were Visual Question Answering in Radiology (VQA-RAD), the Semantically-Labeled Knowledge-Enhanced (SLAKE) data set, and multimodal MedXpertQA (MedXpertQA-MM).\cite{lau2018vqarad,liu2021slake,zuo2025medxpertqa}
