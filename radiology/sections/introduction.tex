\section{Introduction}

Double reading of screening mammograms increased cancer detection compared with a single reader.\cite{taylor2008double} In diagnostic radiology, a systematic review found that a second reader changed reports at rates that depended on the setting.\cite{geijer2018double} Radiologists' errors are perceptual or interpretive: a reader misses an image finding or assigns an incorrect meaning to a finding.\cite{bruno2015error} A second reader adds information only to the extent that the readers do not make the same errors. Pooling independent diagnostic judgments improved accuracy when readers had similar individual accuracy.\cite{kurvers2016pooling} The value of another reader therefore depends on both individual accuracy and error correlation. Artificial intelligence (AI) systems have also been evaluated in the second-reader position, including a simulation of double reading in breast cancer screening.\cite{mckinney2020breast}

Multi-agent systems built from large language models emulate a clinical consultation. Published medical systems assigned clinical roles to several instances of a model, which answered independently before discussing their answers or voting.\cite{kim2024mdagents,tang2024medagents} A reading panel applies this arrangement to questions about radiology images. A large evaluation of agent systems found that the benefit of collaboration shrank as the underlying model became more capable.\cite{kim2026scaling} Another evaluation found correlated errors across language models, including models with different architectures and providers.\cite{correlated2025} In a reading panel built from one vision-language model (VLM), every reader shares the model's weights. These include the visual encoder that extracts image findings and the language model that selects the answer. Sampling and role prompts change the text of each reader's response while leaving the model and its image evidence shared. Different responses alone do not establish independent second reads.

VLMs answer many image questions using information in the question alone. The question-text prior is information supporting an answer from the question and options without image content. In general visual question answering, models exploited language priors; performance decreased when each question was paired with similar images requiring different answers.\cite{goyal2017vqa} This construction required the image to distinguish between answers to the same question. In medical visual question answering, leading multimodal models performed worse than random guessing on simple probing questions about diagnosis.\cite{yan2025worse} Analysis of medical image questions also found correct answers accompanied by flawed image comprehension.\cite{jin2024hidden} Replacing the image cost medical VLMs at most 6.5 percentage points of accuracy on medical benchmarks, against 48 to 61 points on general-domain tasks,\cite{zafar2026see} and a radiology data set constructed to remove such shortcuts reduced text-only accuracy to near chance.\cite{butsanets2026radimagenetvqa} Answer accuracy alone does not measure the contribution of image evidence. In a reading panel, a prior that every reader shares is a common cause of the same error in all of them, and agreement among the readers does not show that each extracted the findings the answer requires.

Whether a reading panel of AI models provides independent second reads of radiology images has not been measured, nor have the sources of its shared errors. The aim of this study was to measure error correlation among AI readers answering radiology questions and determine its sources. We separated image evidence from the question-text prior and seeing from judging. Seeing was defined as the extraction of visual findings, and judging as the selection of an answer using findings and prior knowledge. We measured the contribution of each shared component: VLM, image rendering, role, reference cases, findings report, and discussion. The reader study evaluated four application programming interface (API) VLMs and five open-weight models on five item sets from VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and MedXpertQA.\cite{lau2018vqarad,liu2021slake,zuo2025medxpertqa} Controlled manipulations varied the image through graded evidence, region occlusion, and paired images, and varied what readers shared, including the models used for seeing and judging (Fig. 1).
