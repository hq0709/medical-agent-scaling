\textbf{Purpose:} To measure error correlation among vision-language model (VLM) readers of radiology questions and identify its sources.

\textbf{Material and methods:} Four proprietary VLMs and five open-weight VLMs answered VQA-RAD, SLAKE and MedXpertQA-MM questions. We compared single readers with nine-reader independent, discussion and self-consistency panels (identical prompts, majority vote). Manipulations varied images and shared components. Perception models wrote findings reports; interpretation models answered from them. Outcomes were accuracy, error correlation and effective number of independent readers ($N_{\mathrm{eff}}$), with item bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals.

\textbf{Results:} Qwen 3.8 Flash independent and discussion panels gained 5.6 to 13.6 and 8.8 to 14.0 percentage points over the single reader, respectively. Other proprietary models' panels changed accuracy by $-$2.8 to 4.0 and $-$3.2 to 5.6, respectively. $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ was 1.17 to 1.99. On SLAKE, blank images (uniform gray images of the same size) lowered single-reader accuracy by 25.2 to 40.0 percentage points. On MedXpertQA-MM, 64.2\% to 76.2\% of shared wrong answers (at least seven of nine readers wrong) matched blank-image majorities. Gemini 3.7 Flash reports raised accuracy by 6.1 to 16.3 percentage points relative to the other interpretation models reading the images themselves. Shared-report error correlation exceeded own-report values (0.800 to 0.990 versus 0.641 to 0.818). Roles lowered error correlation by 0.033 to 0.060 versus 0.470 to 0.492 with different models. Readers changed 41.5\% to 100.0\% of correct initial minority answers to wrong answers.

\textbf{Conclusions:} Readers built from one VLM made correlated errors. A second reading lowered error correlation most when it came from a different model.
