\textbf{Purpose:} To measure error correlation among model readers and identify the sources of shared errors on radiology questions.

\textbf{Materials and methods:} We evaluated four application programming interface vision-language models (VLMs) and five open-weight models on five item sets from VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and MedXpertQA. We compared single readers, independent reading panels, and discussion panels. Manipulations included gray images, reduced resolution, region occlusion, image pairs, question-blind findings reports, and shared or private reference cases. Perceivers wrote reports; reasoners answered questions from them. Outcomes were error correlation and the effective number of independent readers. We calculated item bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals.

\textbf{Results:} Panels of nine readers changed accuracy by $-$2.8 to 4.0 percentage points for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5; Qwen 3.8 Flash gained 5.6 to 13.6 percentage points. Error correlation in generic panels was 0.442 to 0.915. Gray images reduced SLAKE single-reader accuracy by 25.2 to 40.0 percentage points; 64.2\% to 76.2\% of MedXpertQA-MM consensus errors repeated gray-image majorities. Gemini 3.7 Flash perceiver reports improved other reasoners' accuracy by 6.1 to 16.3 percentage points; shared-report error correlation was 0.800 to 0.990. On VQA-RAD, error correlation fell by 0.048 with roles versus 0.470 with different models. Discussion overturned 41.5\% to 100.0\% of correct minority answers.

\textbf{Conclusions:} A reading panel of nine readers built from one VLM provided 1.17 to 1.99 effective independent readers because its readers shared the model's seeing and the question-text prior. An independent second read of radiology images requires a different model to perform the seeing.
