\textbf{Purpose:} To measure error correlation among model readers and identify the sources of shared errors on radiology questions.

\textbf{Materials and methods:} This reader study evaluated four application programming interface vision-language models and five open-weight models on five item sets from VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and MedXpertQA. We compared single readers, independent reading panels, and discussion panels. Manipulations included gray images, reduced resolution, region occlusion, image pairs, question-blind findings reports, and shared or private reference cases. Perceivers wrote reports; reasoners answered questions from them. Outcomes were error correlation and the effective number of independent readers. We calculated item bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals; no hypothesis tests were performed.

\textbf{Results:} Panels of nine readers changed accuracy by $-$2.8 to 4.0 percentage points for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5; Qwen 3.8 Flash gained 5.6--13.6 points. Error correlation in generic panels was 0.442--0.915; specialty panels provided 1.17--1.99 effective readers. Observed majority accuracy minus the beta-binomial prediction averaged $-$0.07 points. Gray images reduced SLAKE single-reader accuracy by 25.2--40.0 points; 64.2\%--76.2\% of MedXpertQA-MM consensus errors repeated gray-image majorities. Gemini perceiver reports improved other reasoners' accuracy by 6.1--16.3 points; shared-report error correlation was 0.800--0.990. On VQA-RAD, error correlation fell by 0.048 with roles versus 0.470 with different models. Discussion overturned 41.5\%--100.0\% of correct minority answers; MedXpertQA-MM correct answers differing from gray-image answers were abandoned 7.2\%--23.6\% of the time.

\textbf{Conclusions:} Shared errors limit the independence of model reading panels. Radiology second reads require evaluation of reader accuracy, image use, and error correlation.
