\section{Results}


\subsection{Reading panels and error correlation}
Panels of nine readers changed accuracy little relative to the single reader for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5. Reading-panel differences ranged from $-$2.8 to 0.8, $-$1.2 to 1.2, and $-$2.8 to 4.0 percentage points, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}a). Among their reading and discussion panels, only the Claude Haiku 4.5 discussion panel on VQA-RAD had a difference CI excluding zero: 5.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.8 to 10.8).

Accuracy gains were larger for Qwen 3.8 Flash reading panels than for the other models' reading panels. The VQA-RAD difference was 13.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 7.6 to 19.6). Differences were 8.0 percentage points (95\% CI, 4.0 to 12.4) on SLAKE and 5.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.4 to 11.2) on MedXpertQA-MM. Discussion-panel differences ranged from 8.8 to 14.0 percentage points, and all their CIs excluded zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}a).

Higher accuracy among Qwen 3.8 Flash panel readers accounted for more of the panel advantage than majority voting did. Mean reader accuracy exceeded single-reader accuracy by 3.1 to 10.3 percentage points; majority voting added 1.1 to 3.3 percentage points relative to those readers. For the other models, majority voting changed accuracy by $-$2.1 to 2.4 percentage points relative to mean reader accuracy (Supplementary Tables).

Panels behaved like few independent readers. The effective number of independent readers ranged from 1.17 to 1.39 for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5. It ranged from 1.46 to 1.99 for Qwen 3.8 Flash (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}b).

Discussion brought reader errors close to complete correlation, while unanimous answers still included errors. Final error correlations ranged from 0.995 to 1.000 for the first three models and from 0.822 to 0.913 for Qwen 3.8 Flash (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}c). Unanimity after discussion ranged from 82.4\% to 100.0\%, and 8.9\% to 70.0\% of unanimous answers were wrong.

The beta-binomial model closely reproduced majority accuracy on yes/no items. Across 108 panels, observed accuracy minus the prediction averaged $-$0.07 percentage points, with an SD of 0.84 percentage points (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}d). The largest absolute difference was 2.59 percentage points. Majority accuracy minus mean reader accuracy ranged from $-$2.1 to 5.6 percentage points across error correlations of 0.44 to 0.99 (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}e).

\subsection{Image evidence and the question-text prior}
Replacing the image with gray lowered accuracy on VQA-RAD and SLAKE. Single-reader accuracy ranged from 60.8\% to 81.2\% with original VQA-RAD images and from 46.8\% to 60.8\% with gray images (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence}a). Corresponding SLAKE ranges were 72.4\% to 91.2\% and 38.8\% to 54.4\%. Paired accuracy differences ranged from 11.2 to 26.8 percentage points on VQA-RAD and from 25.2 to 40.0 percentage points on SLAKE. All single-reader and reading-panel difference CIs excluded zero on both data sets.

Removing the image changed single-reader accuracy little on MedXpertQA-MM for GPT-5 mini, Claude Haiku 4.5, and Qwen 3.8 Flash. Their paired differences ranged from 2.0 to 4.0 percentage points, and all CIs included zero. Gemini 3.7 Flash had a difference of 18.0 percentage points (95\% CI, 12.0 to 23.6). Reading-panel difference CIs excluded zero for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Qwen 3.8 Flash (Supplementary Tables).

Removing the image increased error correlation for Gemini 3.7 Flash, Claude Haiku 4.5, and Qwen 3.8 Flash on VQA-RAD and SLAKE. Their VQA-RAD correlations ranged from 0.439 to 0.731 with original images and from 0.841 to 0.922 with gray images (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence}b). Corresponding SLAKE ranges were 0.529 to 0.785 and 0.831 to 0.982. Error correlation did not change monotonically with image resolution (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence}b).

Claude Haiku 4.5 readers remained highly correlated when gray-image analyses excluded abstentions. Abstentions ranged from 4.3\% to 14.8\% across data sets. Error correlation among answered items was 0.963 (95\% CI, 0.942 to 0.982) on VQA-RAD and 0.931 (95\% CI, 0.901 to 0.956) on SLAKE. It was 0.852 (95\% CI, 0.803 to 0.891) on MedXpertQA-MM.

More reader answers were correct only with the real image than only with the gray image. On VQA-RAD, these shares ranged from 24.1\% to 37.3\% and from 10.4\% to 13.6\%, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}a). Corresponding SLAKE ranges were 36.3\% to 46.4\% and 4.4\% to 15.8\%. On MedXpertQA-MM, they were 8.1\% to 22.3\% and 4.4\% to 7.9\%; 12.3\% to 64.0\% of answers were wrong with both images.

Most consensus errors on MedXpertQA-MM repeated the gray-image majority answer. The share ranged from 64.2\% to 76.2\%, compared with 42.9\% to 54.4\% on VQA-RAD and 14.3\% to 57.9\% on SLAKE (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}b).

\subsection{Use of the queried region and of the image}
API readers changed answers more often after target occlusion than after control occlusion, with the largest difference for Gemini 3.7 Flash. On SLAKE-target, region dependence was 17.1 percentage points (95\% CI, 13.2 to 21.2) for GPT-5 mini (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}a,b). It was 32.9 percentage points (95\% CI, 28.2 to 38.0) for Gemini 3.7 Flash and 3.2 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.8 to 5.7) for Claude Haiku 4.5.

Region dependence was positive across open-weight models, with the LLaVA-1.5 7B interval including zero. Its region dependence was 2.4 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$2.0 to 6.8); all other open-weight model CIs excluded zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}b).

Pair accuracy was highest for Gemini 3.7 Flash among the API models tested. On SLAKE-pairs, both single-reader answers were correct for 59.0\% (95\% CI, 52.0 to 65.5) of pairs with GPT-5 mini (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}c). Rates were 74.0\% (95\% CI, 67.5 to 80.0) with Gemini 3.7 Flash and 38.5\% (95\% CI, 31.5 to 45.0) with Claude Haiku 4.5. Their answers differed between paired images in 62.0\%, 75.0\%, and 42.5\% of pairs, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}c; Supplementary Tables).

\subsection{Seeing and judging}
Reader accuracy depended on both the perceiver and the reasoner when each reader read its own question-blind findings report. Across reasoners, accuracy with Gemini 3.7 Flash reports ranged from 78.7\% to 81.3\% on VQA-RAD and from 84.3\% to 88.2\% on SLAKE (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}a). Corresponding ranges with GPT-5 mini reports were 69.0\% to 70.6\% and 81.0\% to 84.9\%; with Claude Haiku 4.5 reports, 61.6\% to 63.6\% and 76.3\% to 80.2\%.

Gemini 3.7 Flash reports raised accuracy for the other reasoners relative to reading the image. Paired gains ranged from 6.1 percentage points (95\% CI, 1.4 to 10.9) to 16.3 percentage points (95\% CI, 9.9 to 22.6). Claude Haiku 4.5 reports lowered their accuracy, with paired differences ranging from $-$25.9 to $-$0.4 percentage points. Reading a reasoner's own reports improved accuracy with a CI excluding zero only for GPT-5 mini on SLAKE: 4.4 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.9 to 8.2). Gemini 3.7 Flash had a paired difference of $-$9.2 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$13.3 to $-$5.2) with its own reports on MedXpertQA-MM.

The reasoner changed accuracy even with identical reports on MedXpertQA-MM. Gemini 3.7 Flash achieved 60.8\%, 74.5\%, and 57.8\% with GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5 reports, respectively. Claude Haiku 4.5 achieved 30.6\%, 45.6\%, and 27.4\% with the same reports; GPT-5 mini achieved 50.1\%, 62.0\%, and 45.0\%. Image-reading accuracy was 83.7\%, 31.7\%, and 52.9\% for Gemini 3.7 Flash, Claude Haiku 4.5, and GPT-5 mini, respectively.

One shared report produced higher error correlation than separate reports from the same model. Across data sets and reasoners, error correlation ranged from 0.800 to 0.990 with one shared report, from 0.641 to 0.818 with own reports, and from 0.692 to 0.915 with images (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}b).

Reports omitted the queried SLAKE target among both errors and all reports. Omission affected 25.8\% to 47.1\% of errors and 26.5\% to 30.8\% of all reports. With Gemini 3.7 Flash as perceiver, these shares were 33.6\% to 47.1\% and 26.7\%, respectively.

\subsection{What two readers share}
Readers sharing everything retained model-dependent error correlation. Across data sets, baseline correlations ranged from 0.692 to 0.800 for GPT-5 mini, 0.776 to 0.828 for Gemini 3.7 Flash, 0.878 to 0.915 for Claude Haiku 4.5, and 0.442 to 0.629 for Qwen 3.8 Flash (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}a).

Changing shared components lowered mean error correlation in the matched contrasts (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}b). In VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and MedXpertQA-MM order, role contrasts averaged $-$0.048 (95\% CI, $-$0.071 to $-$0.023), $-$0.060 (95\% CI, $-$0.087 to $-$0.036), and $-$0.033 (95\% CI, $-$0.051 to $-$0.014). Rendering contrasts were $-$0.092 (95\% CI, $-$0.123 to $-$0.068), $-$0.094 (95\% CI, $-$0.123 to $-$0.066), and $-$0.045 (95\% CI, $-$0.062 to $-$0.027). Reference-case contrasts were $-$0.110 (95\% CI, $-$0.142 to $-$0.084) and $-$0.146 (95\% CI, $-$0.179 to $-$0.117) on VQA-RAD and SLAKE. Report contrasts were $-$0.199 (95\% CI, $-$0.233 to $-$0.164), $-$0.204 (95\% CI, $-$0.247 to $-$0.162), and $-$0.172 (95\% CI, $-$0.211 to $-$0.142). Perceiver contrasts were $-$0.311 (95\% CI, $-$0.362 to $-$0.260), $-$0.275 (95\% CI, $-$0.334 to $-$0.218), and $-$0.263 (95\% CI, $-$0.294 to $-$0.227). Reasoner contrasts were $-$0.108 (95\% CI, $-$0.132 to $-$0.083), $-$0.167 (95\% CI, $-$0.222 to $-$0.129), and $-$0.430 (95\% CI, $-$0.477 to $-$0.389). Model contrasts were $-$0.470 (95\% CI, $-$0.522 to $-$0.421), $-$0.473 (95\% CI, $-$0.544 to $-$0.406), and $-$0.492 (95\% CI, $-$0.536 to $-$0.445).

Model-specific contrasts varied in direction for roles and rendering. VQA-RAD ranges were $-$0.148 to 0.011 and $-$0.181 to 0.023, respectively. Reference-case contrasts ranged from $-$0.229 to $-$0.043 on VQA-RAD and from $-$0.247 to $-$0.082 on SLAKE. MedXpertQA-MM reasoner contrasts ranged from $-$0.546 to $-$0.310; VQA-RAD model contrasts ranged from $-$0.696 to $-$0.272. The regression summary also associated different perceivers and reasoners with lower error correlation across data sets (Supplementary Tables).

\subsection{Reference cases}
Shared reference cases changed single-reader accuracy differently across models and data sets. On VQA-RAD, Claude Haiku 4.5 accuracy was 60.8\% without references, 68.8\% with reference images, and 61.2\% with reference text (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}a). On SLAKE, its corresponding accuracies were 72.4\%, 67.6\%, and 76.8\%. GPT-5 mini and Gemini 3.7 Flash achieved 73.6\% and 82.4\% with reference images on VQA-RAD, compared with 75.6\% and 83.6\% with reference text. Corresponding SLAKE accuracies were 79.2\% and 89.2\% with images, and 81.6\% and 90.8\% with text.

Private reference cases lowered error correlation relative to shared references in every model and reference format, while panel accuracy varied (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}b,c). On SLAKE, GPT-5 mini panel accuracy was 81.6\% with shared reference images and 84.0\% with private reference images; error correlation was 0.741 and 0.667, respectively. Gemini 3.7 Flash panel accuracy was 90.4\% with shared reference text and 92.0\% with private reference text; error correlation was 0.823 and 0.716.

Claude Haiku 4.5 private references lowered error correlation without raising panel accuracy in either format or data set. With SLAKE reference images, error correlation was 0.928 with shared references and 0.655 with private references, while panel accuracy was 70.4\% and 68.8\%. With reference text, error correlation was 0.941 and 0.720, while panel accuracy remained 78.0\%.

\subsection{Discussion among readers}
On MedXpertQA-MM, readers abandoned correct answers that differed from their gray-image answer more often than correct answers that matched it. Abandonment ranged from 7.2\% to 23.6\% for the former and from 2.4\% to 6.3\% for the latter (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}a). Paired differences ranged from 4.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.8 to 9.0) for Gemini 3.7 Flash to 17.4 percentage points (95\% CI, 6.4 to 29.7) for Claude Haiku 4.5. All paired difference CIs excluded zero.

The difference in abandonment was smaller on VQA-RAD and SLAKE. GPT-5 mini had difference CIs excluding zero: 5.1 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.4 to 10.4) and 2.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.3 to 5.2), respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}a).

Readers abandoned correct minority answers more often than correct majority answers. Across models and data sets, abandonment ranged from 41.5\% to 100.0\% for answers initially held by a minority and from 1.0\% to 6.5\% for majority answers (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}b).

Correct answers differing from the gray-image answer were more often in the initial minority on MedXpertQA-MM than on the other data sets. The share ranged from 6.6\% to 23.5\%, compared with 2.5\% to 11.2\% on VQA-RAD and 1.8\% to 4.0\% on SLAKE (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}c).

\subsection{Open-weight models}
Open-weight models lost accuracy without the image on VQA-RAD and SLAKE, while MedXpertQA-MM accuracy stayed near chance. Original-image accuracy ranged from 56.0\% to 80.0\% on VQA-RAD and from 66.0\% to 88.8\% on SLAKE (Supplementary Fig.~S1a). Gray-image ranges were 54.0\% to 60.8\% and 40.0\% to 50.8\%, respectively. On MedXpertQA-MM, original-image accuracy ranged from 20.0\% to 24.4\%, compared with 20.0\% to 23.6\% with gray images. MedGemma 4B had the highest original-image accuracy on VQA-RAD and SLAKE; Qwen3-VL 8B had the highest on MedXpertQA-MM.

Visual reliance varied across open-weight models. Mean absolute change in log-odds of yes ranged from 0.89 to 6.43 on VQA-RAD and from 1.40 to 11.23 on SLAKE (Supplementary Fig.~S1b). On MedXpertQA-MM, mean visual reliance measured by Kullback--Leibler divergence ranged from 0.10 to 0.88.

Models sharing a vision encoder did not have consistently higher error correlation than models with different encoders. For HuatuoGPT-Vision 7B and LLaVA-1.5 7B, error correlation divided by its maximum given marginal error rates ranged from 0.213 to 0.451 (Supplementary Fig.~S1c). Values for pairs with different encoders ranged from 0.148 to 0.725.

API consensus errors were associated with lower open-weight visual reliance on SLAKE, with little association on the other data sets. Spearman correlations were $-$0.048 on VQA-RAD, $-$0.401 on SLAKE, and 0.006 on MedXpertQA-MM.
