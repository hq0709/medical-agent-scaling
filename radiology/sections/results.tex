\section{Results}


\subsection{Reading panels and error correlation}
For GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5, independent panels changed accuracy by $-$2.8 to 4.0 percentage points relative to the single reader. Model-specific ranges were $-$2.8 to 0.8, $-$1.2 to 1.2, and $-$2.8 to 4.0 percentage points, respectively (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}a). Their discussion-panel differences ranged from $-$3.2 to 5.6 percentage points. The Claude Haiku 4.5 discussion panel increased accuracy on VQA-RAD by 5.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.8 to 10.8).

Qwen 3.8 Flash independent panels increased accuracy on VQA-RAD by 13.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 7.6 to 19.6). Gains were 8.0 percentage points (95\% CI, 4.0 to 12.4) on SLAKE and 5.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.4 to 11.2) on MedXpertQA-MM. Discussion-panel gains ranged from 8.8 to 14.0 percentage points (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}a; Supplementary Tables S2--S5).

For Qwen 3.8 Flash, mean reader accuracy exceeded single-reader accuracy by 3.1 to 10.3 percentage points; majority voting added 1.1 to 3.3 percentage points. For the other models, majority voting changed accuracy by $-$2.1 to 2.4 percentage points relative to mean reader accuracy (Supplementary Tables S2--S5).

Panels of nine readers had an $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of 1.17 to 1.99 (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}b). Values ranged from 1.17 to 1.39 for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5, and from 1.46 to 1.99 for Qwen 3.8 Flash.

After discussion, error correlations were 0.995 to 1.000 for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5 (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}c). Qwen 3.8 Flash correlations were 0.822 to 0.913. Unanimity after discussion ranged from 82.4\% to 100.0\%, and 8.9\% to 70.0\% of unanimous answers were wrong.

Across 108 panels on yes/no items, observed majority accuracy minus the beta-binomial prediction averaged $-$0.07 percentage points (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}d). The standard deviation (SD) was 0.84 percentage points, and the largest absolute difference was 2.59 percentage points. Majority accuracy minus mean reader accuracy ranged from $-$2.1 to 5.6 percentage points across error correlations of 0.44 to 0.99 (Fig.~\ref{fig:panels}e).

\subsection{Visual evidence and the language prior}
Blank images lowered single-reader accuracy by 11.2 to 26.8 percentage points on VQA-RAD and 25.2 to 40.0 percentage points on SLAKE (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence}a). Accuracy ranged from 60.8\% to 81.2\% with original VQA-RAD images and from 46.8\% to 60.8\% with blank images. Corresponding SLAKE ranges were 72.4\% to 91.2\% and 38.8\% to 54.4\%. Independent-panel accuracy also decreased on both data sets (Supplementary Tables S6--S8).

On MedXpertQA-MM, original-image minus blank-image single-reader accuracy was 4.0 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$2.0 to 10.0) for GPT-5 mini. It was 3.6 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$0.4 to 8.0) for Claude Haiku 4.5. Qwen 3.8 Flash had a difference of 2.0 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$4.4 to 8.0). With blank images, Gemini 3.7 Flash accuracy decreased by 18.0 percentage points (95\% CI, 12.0 to 23.6). Independent-panel accuracy decreased for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Qwen 3.8 Flash (Supplementary Tables S6--S8).

For Gemini 3.7 Flash, Claude Haiku 4.5, and Qwen 3.8 Flash, blank-image error correlations were 0.841 to 0.922 on VQA-RAD and 0.831 to 0.982 on SLAKE (Fig.~\ref{fig:evidence}b). Corresponding original-image ranges were 0.439 to 0.731 and 0.529 to 0.785. On VQA-RAD, GPT-5 mini error correlation fell from 0.752 with original images to 0.694 at 256 pixels and 0.644 at 128 pixels. Qwen 3.8 Flash correlations rose from 0.439 to 0.442 and 0.480, respectively.

After abstentions were excluded, Claude Haiku 4.5 blank-image error correlation was 0.963 (95\% CI, 0.942 to 0.982) on VQA-RAD. It was 0.931 (95\% CI, 0.901 to 0.956) on SLAKE and 0.852 (95\% CI, 0.803 to 0.891) on MedXpertQA-MM. Abstentions ranged from 4.3\% to 14.8\% across data sets.

On VQA-RAD, 24.1\% to 37.3\% of reader answers were correct only with the original image, compared with 10.4\% to 13.6\% only with the blank image (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}a). Corresponding SLAKE ranges were 36.3\% to 46.4\% and 4.4\% to 15.8\%. On MedXpertQA-MM, they were 8.1\% to 22.3\% and 4.4\% to 7.9\%; 12.3\% to 64.0\% of answers were wrong with both images.

On MedXpertQA-MM, 64.2\% to 76.2\% of shared wrong answers matched the blank-image majority answer (Fig.~\ref{fig:prior}b). The corresponding shares were 42.9\% to 54.4\% on VQA-RAD and 14.3\% to 57.9\% on SLAKE.

\subsection{Occlusion sensitivity and paired images}
On SLAKE-target, occlusion sensitivity was 17.1 percentage points (95\% CI, 13.2 to 21.2) for GPT-5 mini (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}a,b). It was 32.9 percentage points (95\% CI, 28.2 to 38.0) for Gemini 3.7 Flash. Claude Haiku 4.5 occlusion sensitivity was 3.2 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.8 to 5.7).

Open-weight occlusion sensitivity ranged from 2.4 to 21.6 percentage points (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}b; Supplementary Tables S11--S12). The LLaVA-1.5 7B estimate was 2.4 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$2.0 to 6.8). Target occlusion increased answer changes relative to control occlusion for the other four models.

Gemini 3.7 Flash had the highest paired-image accuracy among proprietary models: 74.0\% (95\% CI, 67.5 to 80.0) on SLAKE-pairs (Fig.~\ref{fig:region}c). Both single-reader answers were correct for 59.0\% (95\% CI, 52.0 to 65.5) of pairs with GPT-5 mini. The rate was 38.5\% (95\% CI, 31.5 to 45.0) with Claude Haiku 4.5. Answers differed between paired images in 62.0\%, 75.0\%, and 42.5\% of pairs for GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5, respectively (Supplementary Tables S13--S14).

\subsection{Perception and interpretation}
In the own-report condition, Gemini 3.7 Flash reports yielded 78.7\% to 81.3\% accuracy on VQA-RAD and 84.3\% to 88.2\% on SLAKE across interpretation models (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}a). Corresponding ranges with GPT-5 mini reports were 69.0\% to 70.6\% and 81.0\% to 84.9\%. With Claude Haiku 4.5 reports, they were 61.6\% to 63.6\% and 76.3\% to 80.2\%.

With Gemini 3.7 Flash reports, accuracy gains ranged from 6.1 percentage points (95\% CI, 1.4 to 10.9) to 16.3 percentage points (95\% CI, 9.9 to 22.6). These gains were relative to the other interpretation models reading the images themselves. Claude Haiku 4.5 reports lowered the other interpretation models' accuracy in 5 of 6 cells, by 7.9 to 25.9 percentage points. The sixth difference, for GPT-5 mini on SLAKE, was $-$0.4 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$5.0 to 4.6). Accuracy with same-model reports increased only for GPT-5 mini on SLAKE: 4.4 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.9 to 8.2). Gemini 3.7 Flash had a paired difference of $-$9.2 percentage points (95\% CI, $-$13.3 to $-$5.2) with same-model reports on MedXpertQA-MM.

On MedXpertQA-MM, Gemini 3.7 Flash achieved 60.8\%, 74.5\%, and 57.8\% accuracy with GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.7 Flash, and Claude Haiku 4.5 reports, respectively. Claude Haiku 4.5 achieved 30.6\%, 45.6\%, and 27.4\% with the same reports; GPT-5 mini achieved 50.1\%, 62.0\%, and 45.0\%. Image-reading accuracy was 83.7\%, 31.7\%, and 52.9\% for Gemini 3.7 Flash, Claude Haiku 4.5, and GPT-5 mini, respectively.

With same-model reports, error correlations were 0.800 to 0.990 in the shared-report condition, compared with 0.641 to 0.818 in the own-report condition (Fig.~\ref{fig:report}b). Image-reading correlations ranged from 0.692 to 0.915. Shared-report correlation exceeded image-reading correlation except for Claude Haiku 4.5 on MedXpertQA-MM (0.893 versus 0.915).

The queried SLAKE target was omitted in 25.8\% to 47.1\% of reports behind an error, compared with 26.5\% to 30.8\% of all reports. With Gemini 3.7 Flash as the perception model, omission was more frequent among reports behind errors (33.6\% to 47.1\%) than among all reports (26.7\%).

\subsection{Error correlation by shared component}
For self-consistency readers, error correlation ranged from 0.442 to 0.629 for Qwen 3.8 Flash and from 0.692 to 0.800 for GPT-5 mini (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}a). Ranges were 0.776 to 0.828 for Gemini 3.7 Flash and 0.878 to 0.915 for Claude Haiku 4.5.

Changing the model lowered mean error correlation by 0.470 to 0.492 across data sets (Fig.~\ref{fig:budget}b; Supplementary Tables S20--S22 give estimates with 95\% CIs). Perception-model contrasts ranged from $-$0.311 to $-$0.263, and interpretation-model contrasts from $-$0.430 to $-$0.108. Within-model contrasts ranged from $-$0.060 to $-$0.033 for role and from $-$0.094 to $-$0.045 for contrast adjustment. Ranges were $-$0.146 to $-$0.110 for reference cases and $-$0.204 to $-$0.172 for report.

On VQA-RAD, per-model contrasts ranged from $-$0.148 to 0.011 for role and from $-$0.181 to 0.023 for contrast adjustment. Reference-case contrasts ranged from $-$0.229 to $-$0.043 on VQA-RAD and from $-$0.247 to $-$0.082 on SLAKE. MedXpertQA-MM interpretation-model contrasts ranged from $-$0.546 to $-$0.310; VQA-RAD model contrasts ranged from $-$0.696 to $-$0.272.

In the regression, an additive secondary summary, the VQA-RAD report coefficient was 0.150 (95\% CI, 0.092 to 0.218), compared with a matched contrast of $-$0.199. Different perception and interpretation models were associated with lower error correlation across data sets (Supplementary Tables S23--S24).

\subsection{Reference cases}
On VQA-RAD, Claude Haiku 4.5 accuracy was 60.8\% without reference cases, 68.8\% with reference images, and 61.2\% with reference text (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}a). On SLAKE, its corresponding accuracies were 72.4\%, 67.6\%, and 76.8\%. GPT-5 mini and Gemini 3.7 Flash achieved 73.6\% and 82.4\% with reference images on VQA-RAD, compared with 75.6\% and 83.6\% with reference text. Corresponding SLAKE accuracies were 79.2\% and 89.2\% with images, and 81.6\% and 90.8\% with text.

Private reference cases lowered error correlation in every model and format, from 0.741 to 0.667 for GPT-5 mini on SLAKE with reference images (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}c). Its panel accuracy was 81.6\% with shared reference images and 84.0\% with private reference images (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}b). Gemini 3.7 Flash panel accuracy was 90.4\% with shared reference text and 92.0\% with private reference text; error correlation was 0.823 and 0.716.

For Claude Haiku 4.5, private reference cases yielded equal or lower panel accuracy in both formats and data sets (Fig.~\ref{fig:memory}b). With SLAKE reference images, error correlation was 0.928 with shared cases and 0.655 with private cases; panel accuracy was 70.4\% and 68.8\%, respectively. With reference text, error correlation was 0.941 and 0.720, while panel accuracy remained 78.0\%.

\subsection{Discussion rounds}
On MedXpertQA-MM, conformity ranged from 7.2\% to 23.6\% when correct initial answers differed from the blank-image answer, compared with 2.6\% to 6.3\% when they matched it (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}a). Paired differences ranged from 4.6 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.8 to 9.0) for Gemini 3.7 Flash to 17.4 percentage points (95\% CI, 6.4 to 29.7) for Claude Haiku 4.5.

On VQA-RAD, conformity was 5.1 percentage points higher (95\% CI, 0.4 to 10.4) for GPT-5 mini when initial answers differed from the blank-image answer (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}a). The difference was 5.9 percentage points (95\% CI, 0.6 to 11.5) for Qwen 3.8 Flash. On SLAKE, conformity was 2.6 percentage points higher (95\% CI, 0.3 to 5.2) for GPT-5 mini.

Readers changed 41.5\% to 100.0\% of their correct initial minority answers to a wrong answer, compared with 1.0\% to 6.5\% of correct initial majority answers (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}b).

On MedXpertQA-MM, 6.6\% to 23.5\% of correct initial answers that differed from the blank-image answer were in the initial minority (Fig.~\ref{fig:conformity}c). The corresponding shares were 2.5\% to 11.2\% on VQA-RAD and 1.8\% to 4.0\% on SLAKE.

\subsection{Open-weight models}
With blank images, four of five open-weight models lost 16.4 to 19.2 percentage points on VQA-RAD; LLaVA-1.5 7B lost 2.0 percentage points. All five lost 16.0 to 48.8 percentage points on SLAKE. Original-image accuracy ranged from 56.0\% to 80.0\% on VQA-RAD and from 66.0\% to 88.8\% on SLAKE (Supplementary Fig.~S1a). Blank-image ranges were 54.0\% to 60.8\% and 40.0\% to 50.8\%, respectively. On MedXpertQA-MM, original-image accuracy ranged from 20.0\% to 24.4\%, compared with 20.0\% to 23.6\% with blank images. MedGemma 4B had the highest original-image accuracy on VQA-RAD and SLAKE; Qwen3-VL 8B had the highest on MedXpertQA-MM.

Mean absolute change in log-odds of yes ranged from 0.89 to 6.43 on VQA-RAD and from 1.40 to 11.23 on SLAKE (Supplementary Fig.~S1b). On MedXpertQA-MM, mean visual reliance measured by Kullback--Leibler divergence ranged from 0.10 to 0.88.

HuatuoGPT-Vision 7B and LLaVA-1.5 7B, the pair sharing a vision encoder, had error correlation divided by its maximum given marginal error rates of 0.213 to 0.451 (Supplementary Fig.~S1c). Values for pairs with different encoders ranged from 0.148 to 0.725.

The Spearman correlation between shared wrong answers of proprietary models and open-weight visual reliance was $-$0.401 (95\% CI, $-$0.502 to $-$0.295) on SLAKE. It was $-$0.048 (95\% CI, $-$0.165 to 0.068) on VQA-RAD and 0.006 (95\% CI, $-$0.128 to 0.136) on MedXpertQA-MM.
