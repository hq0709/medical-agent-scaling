# Writing review: "Reading panels of vision-language models share their errors" (Meta-Radiology draft)

Base path: /home/myid/hj67104/consultation_saturation/paper/radiology/. Each paragraph in the .tex sources is one line, so file:line points to a paragraph. Quotes are verbatim LaTeX; citation commands are shown as [cite]. Every number was checked against numbers.md; "derived" marks a value I computed from numbers.md, not one the manuscript reports. ## Findings (most important first)

1. A-01 | sections/abstract.tex:7; sections/discussion.tex:19; sections/conclusions.tex:3 | "An independent second read of radiology images requires a different model to perform the seeing." | Over-claiming (2.1; Layer 4, verb stronger than evidence). "Requires" is a necessity claim that no condition tested, and "independent" overstates the result: readers from different models still correlate (derived from numbers.md C, one-model baseline plus model contrast: about 0.13 to 0.46 per model pair). The largest drop comes from a different model (−0.470 to −0.492), which changes perception and answer selection together. The perceiver-only contrast is −0.263 to −0.311, and on MedXpertQA-MM the reasoner contrast (−0.430) exceeds it. Discussion 19 and Conclusions 3 carry the same claim ("adds independent information when a different model performs the seeing"). | Suggest: "Readers from different models had 0.470 to 0.492 lower error correlation than readers of one model, more than any within-model change (at most 0.204)." | must-fix

Three most recurrent patterns:
1. Repeated emphasis. The Discussion re-reports the Results: topic sentences, the same numbers, and the Introduction's citations. Its first paragraph and last sentence also duplicate the Conclusions; the last sentence is word for word. Five refrains each recur four to seven times:
   - one model is about one read;
   - panel value depends on accuracy plus correlation;
   - different models reduce correlation more than within-model changes;
   - a shared report raises error correlation;
   - discussion overturns correct minority answers.
2. Invented or drifting names for single constructs. The coinages are seeing/judging, question-text prior, graded evidence, rendering, generic panel, region dependence and region reliance, and report pathway. The conformity outcome has five names, and "independent" carries four meanings. The standard terms are already in the paper's own citations: perceptual and interpretive (Bruno 2015), language priors (Goyal 2017), automation bias (Dratsch 2023), and Independent, Centralized, Decentralized and Hybrid (Kim et al. 2026).
3. Wording that goes beyond or beside the numbers. Examples are "requires", "because", "explain why", "increased" and "the same", used on untested or small differences. In the abstract, numbers appear without their comparator or for only one data set. There are three number defects: 2.4 where the source gives 2.6; S5 values that are absent from numbers.md; and a 120-word cap against a 127-word maximum.

Checks with no findings: no em-dashes; no stock AI vocabulary (delve, underscore, pivotal, leverage and the like); no sentence-initial Moreover/Furthermore/Additionally; no "Notably", "Importantly" or "It is worth noting"; no "novel" or "to the best of our knowledge"; no citation dumping; no contribution-list cliches.
