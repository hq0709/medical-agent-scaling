Medical language-model systems use multi-agent consultation, prompting agents into clinical
specialties to discuss cases and reach a shared answer. This design relies on specialists making
different mistakes. In this work, we measure their error correlation across $480$ multi-agent
configurations and $161{,}999$ clinical episodes. At nine members, an Independent panel is
unanimous on $67.5\%$ of items and wrong on $38.1\%$ of what it agrees on. Errors correlate at
$\varphi = 0.76$, giving nine agents $1.27$ effective independent opinions, falling to $1.01$
after discussion. Mixing models from six vendors yields $2.29$ effective opinions. Two published medical agent frameworks, run from their own code, have
error correlations of $0.860$ and $0.863$. In simpler terms: a panel of nine is worth one, and it
agrees anyway; its agreement must not reach a clinician as confidence.
