\section{Model capability index}
\label{app:capability}

\citet{kim2026scaling} score models on a published Intelligence Index and report that a
\emph{task-grounded} capability metric explains more variance ($R^2 = 0.413$) than the generic one
($R^2 = 0.373$). We define the task-grounded capability index $I$ as a model's mean
single-doctor chain-of-thought accuracy across the three benchmarks. Table~\ref{tab:capability}
gives the components. The three OpenAI capability tiers span $I = 34.0$ to $59.2$, and their single-benchmark
baselines span 15.6\% to 91.6\%, crossing the 45\% region from both sides.

\section{Domain complexity}
\label{app:complexity}

\subsection{Complexity metric construction}
Domain complexity $D \in [0,1]$ is the arithmetic mean of three complementary
measures~\citep{kim2026scaling}: the \textbf{performance ceiling} $1 - p_{\max}$, where $p_{\max}$ is the
highest performance any evaluated system reaches; the \textbf{coefficient of variation}
$\sigma/\mu$ of performance across all configurations, a scale-invariant measure of relative
spread; and the \textbf{best-model baseline} $1 - p_{\text{best}}$, where $p_{\text{best}}$ is the
strongest single-model performance on the dataset.

\subsection{Domain characterisation}
Table~\ref{tab:complexity} gives the components. MedQA is a low-complexity domain ($D = 0.060$);
MedXpertQA and MedAgentsBench-hard have similar, higher complexity values
($D = 0.370$ and $0.392$).

\end{table*}

\subsection{The critical threshold does not transfer}
A critical complexity threshold at $D \approx 0.40$ has been reported for general-domain
agents~\citep{kim2026scaling}: below it
multi-agent architectures yield net positive returns, above it coordination overhead consumes
resources otherwise spent on reasoning. MedXpertQA ($D = 0.370$) and MedAgentsBench-hard ($D = 0.392$) differ by $0.022$ in
complexity yet by $1.8$ pp in mean multi-agent gain ($+1.44$ pp versus $-0.34$ pp), giving
opposite signs below the reported threshold. Gains also vary \emph{within} each benchmark: on MedAgentsBench-hard the
per-tier gains run $-2.78$, $+1.02$, $+0.66$ pp as $\PSA$ moves from 20.4\% through 32.4\% to
44.4\%. The configuration-level baseline $\PSA$ distinguishes these gains.

\section{Datasets}
\label{app:data}

\textbf{MedXpertQA-Text}~\citep{zuo2025medxpertqa}: 2{,}450 expert-level items, ten options each,
tagged by 12 body systems and 3 task types. We draw a stratified sample interleaved across
(body system $\times$ task) so that any prefix is approximately balanced, then take the first 250.
\textbf{MedQA-USMLE}~\citep{jin2021medqa}: 1{,}273 four-option test items; randomly shuffled with a
fixed seed, first 250. \textbf{MedAgentsBench-hard}~\citep{medagentsbench}: the
contamination-screened hard split, 894 items after excluding the \texttt{medqa\_5options} subset,
stratified-interleaved across the ten source subsets, first 250. All samples are nested prefixes,
so a larger sample re-uses every call of a smaller one.

Item difficulty is additionally tagged easy/medium/hard by the pass rate of three mid-tier models
at $k{=}5$; on the MedXpertQA sample this yields 13/62/125 items.

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}

\subsection{Technical infrastructure}
All models are accessed through one OpenAI-compatible client with a content-addressed disk cache,
exponential backoff with rate-limit-aware retry, a per-call JSONL ledger recording tokens, latency
and cost, and a cross-process spend journal guarded by an OS file lock. Every LLM call is cached
on the full request (model, system, messages, temperature, seed, max tokens, effort, JSON mode,
tag), so a re-run is a replay.

\subsection{Agent configuration}
Single-doctor baselines answer in one call. Independent panels deploy $N$ specialists with
majority-vote aggregation, ties broken by mean stated confidence and then option order.
Centralized systems use one orchestrator over $N$ sub-agents with a
maximum of 2 orchestration rounds and one re-task per round. Decentralized systems run $N$ agents
through up to 3 discussion rounds, stopping early only on unanimity. Hybrid systems combine a
generalist triage gate with the specialist panel and escalate to full discussion when the panel
has no majority. Agent temperature is 0.7 within panels, 0.3 for single-doctor baselines and for
orchestrator/triage turns. Reasoning models ignore temperature and are called at \texttt{effort=low} with a minimum
completion budget of 1{,}600 tokens, since reasoning tokens are drawn from the same budget and a
smaller cap returns empty output.

\subsection{Prompt compilation}
Every agent receives the same rendered item (stem, then options as \texttt{(A) ...}) and the same
answer schema: JSON with \texttt{answer}, \texttt{confidence} (0--100) and \texttt{reason}
(at most 45 words). For specialist panel members, the system prompt names their specialty. Peer context in
communicating architectures is capped at a fixed 900-character budget, balanced-truncated across
peers and \emph{independent of $N$}, so a panel-size effect cannot be a context-length effect.
Peer ordering is deterministically rotated per (item, round) so that the first-listed speaker is
decorrelated from the top-ranked specialty.

\section{The published scaling form, fitted to medical data}
\label{app:scaling}

The main text measures collaboration gain directly. We also fit the scaling form proposed for
general-domain agent systems~\citep{kim2026scaling} to the OpenAI grid.

\paragraph{With accuracy as the response, the fit is degenerate.}
Fitting that form directly gives $R^2 = 0.997$ (5-fold
cross-validated $R^2 = 0.996$), which is an artefact of our design rather than a result. The
response is accuracy and one predictor is the single-doctor baseline $\PSA$; the same items and
the same model underlie both terms, so $\PSA$ alone nearly determines accuracy
($\hat\beta_{\PSA} = 0.867$). We therefore refit with collaboration
\emph{gain} as the response.

\paragraph{Fitting collaboration gain.}
With $\mathrm{gain} = P - \PSA$ as the response, $R^2 = 0.528$ and 5-fold cross-validated
$R^2 = 0.440$. The coefficients below are fitted to $n = 225$ OpenAI configurations,
including the self-consistency control.

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
\toprule
term & $\hat\beta$ \\
\midrule
$\log(1+\Ae)$ & $-0.150$ \\
$R$ (redundancy) & $+0.084$ \\
$c$ (message density) & $+0.006$ \\
$\log(1+O\%)$ & $+0.005$ \\
$\PSA \times \log(1{+}n_a)$ & $+0.029$ \\
$\log(1+n_a)$ & $-0.005$ \\
$\PSA$ & $-0.113$ \\
$\PSA^2$ & $+0.031$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Error amplification has a negative coefficient, while redundancy, message density and
overhead have positive coefficients.

$T_{\text{tools}} = 0$ throughout, so the $\log(1+T)$ main effect and the
$O\%\times T$ and $\Ec\times T$ interactions vanish identically.

\paragraph{Robustness.}
Capability in this regression is each tier's MedQA probe accuracy, which differs from $I$ defined above.
The linear and quadratic capability coefficients have opposite signs ($+0.302$ and $-0.078$).
With three capability tiers the quadratic is unidentifiable and we do not interpret it.

Regularised alternatives do not improve prediction: Lasso (10-fold CV for $\lambda$) retains 9 of 11
predictors at CV $R^2 = 0.403$ and Ridge reaches $0.165$, both below the unregularised $0.440$, so
we retain the full specification.

In nested model comparisons (5-fold CV $R^2$), the full model reaches $0.440$ and capability alone $0.206$.
Coordination metrics alone reach $0.095$, the single-doctor baseline alone $0.051$ and
panel size alone $-0.007$, worse than predicting the mean.

Figure~\ref{fig:coord} plots all three quantities.

\paragraph{Turns grow sub-linearly; message density explains little variance.}
Fitting $T = a(n+0.5)^{b}$ gives $a = 0.84$, $b = 0.784$, $R^2 = 0.332$. The exponent is
sub-linear, so turns grow more slowly than agents. Turn counts by architecture are
Decentralized $7.1$, Centralized $6.2$, Independent $5.0$, Hybrid $2.2$, against the single doctor's
$1.4$, a range of $1.5$ to $5.1\times$.


$S = 0.510 - 0.026\ln(c)$, $R^2 = 0.021$: message density explains almost none of the variance in
accuracy. Our Centralized panels run at $c = 0.87$ and Decentralized at $c = 0.68$, above the
$c^{*} = 0.39$ plateau reported by \citet{kim2026scaling}.

\paragraph{Coordination failure needs a channel to occur on.}
We classify first-round errors into four categories, following the multi-agent failure
taxonomy of \citet{mast2025}:

\begin{center}\footnotesize
\setlength{\tabcolsep}{3.5pt}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
& Indep. & Centr. & Decentr. & Hybrid \\
\midrule
Logical contradiction & 5.4\% & 5.4\% & 5.4\% & 3.2\% \\
Numerical drift & 5.8\% & 5.8\% & 5.8\% & 4.0\% \\
Context omission & 13.8\% & 13.8\% & 13.8\% & 7.5\% \\
Coordination failure & \textbf{0.0\%} & 2.1\% & 0.8\% & 0.0\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Coordination failure is $0.0\%$ under Independent coordination: with
$C = \emptyset$ there is no channel on which it could occur. Hybrid is also at $0.0\%$.
\citet{kim2026scaling} report that peer verification cuts logical contradiction from $16.8\%$ to $11.5\%$;
our first-round contradiction rate is $5.4\%$ across communicating and
non-communicating architectures. Numerical drift is rarer than in
tool-using tasks ($4$--$6\%$ against $19$--$26\%$).

\section{Reproducing the two published frameworks}
\label{app:frameworks}

Both frameworks are implemented from their authors' code, not from the papers'
prose. MedAgents' twelve prompt functions are copied verbatim from
\texttt{prompt\_generator.py} in \texttt{gersteinlab/MedAgents} and its control flow
follows \texttt{fully\_decode()}'s \texttt{syn\_verif} branch, including
\texttt{max\_attempt\_vote}${=}3$ and the \texttt{cleansing\_syn\_report} step that
prepends the question and options to the synthesised report. MDAgents follows
\texttt{determine\_difficulty} and \texttt{process\_intermediate\_query} from
\texttt{mitmedialab/MDAgents}, with the three-level complexity prompt quoted exactly
and the early stop that ends a round when no agent wishes to address another.

We deviate from the official code in three ways. MDAgents runs two turns per round against the
official five, which reduces cost by roughly a factor of four and does not touch the
first-round opinions $\varphi$ is computed on. Both frameworks run on
\texttt{gpt-4.1-nano}; their official prompts allocate 30 to 50 tokens to short
outputs such as a yes/no vote, and a reasoning model spends that budget entirely on
thinking and returns nothing, so a non-reasoning model is both workable and closer to
the GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 the original papers used. MedAgents' published pipeline
produces domain \emph{analyses} rather than option choices at the point where we need
per-expert answers, so each expert is additionally asked for a letter; that call sits
outside the official control flow and does not affect the framework's output.

Answers are parsed by the explicit formats the prompts request. A fallback that
accepted a bare capital letter had to be removed: MedXpertQA has ten options, so
\texttt{I} is a legal answer and a sentence beginning ``I \ldots'' was read as one.
Raw responses are stored with every episode so a parsing question can be settled
without rerunning.

\subsection{Where capability belongs inside a panel}
\label{app:hetero}

A separate arm varies the capability of the panel's members (Fig.~\ref{fig:hetero}).

\paragraph{Specialist capability matters four times more than the orchestrator's.}
Replacing the whole panel with weak models reduces accuracy by $34.4$ pp (49.2\% to 14.8\%). Splitting that
substitution isolates where the loss comes from. Downgrading only the \emph{orchestrator}, keeping
strong specialists, reduces accuracy by $3.6$ pp, so the panel retains 90\% of the homogeneous-high span.
Downgrading only the \emph{specialists}, keeping a strong orchestrator, reduces accuracy by $14.8$ pp, retaining
57\%. The asymmetry is $4.1\times$.

\paragraph{A weak orchestrator overrides a correct specialist majority.}
The largest single degradation anywhere in this study is a Centralized panel with a weak model on
MedQA at $\PSA = 66.0\%$: $-13.60$ pp. In those episodes the orchestrator overrides a correct
specialist majority.

\paragraph{Mixed-capability panels score below homogeneous strong panels.}
A panel of one strong, one mid and one weak specialist reaches 40.8\% under Centralized and 44.0\%
under Decentralized, against 49.2\% and 47.6\% for homogeneous-high. Both sit below the strong
homogeneous panel and well above the weak one, roughly where a capability-weighted average would
put them. In one model family, \citet{kim2026scaling} report that a low-capability orchestrator with
high-capability sub-agents outperformed a homogeneous high-capability panel by 31\%. In our data
that configuration is the second-best Centralized cell but still $7\%$ below homogeneous-high,
directionally matching what they observe for OpenAI models, where heterogeneous Centralized
configurations degrade.
