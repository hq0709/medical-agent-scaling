\section{Conclusion}

A panel of nine language-model specialists is worth one physician. Error correlation and capture
rate $\kappa$ identify two constraints on consultation gains.

Role-prompted specialists' errors correlate at $\varphi = 0.758$, leaving a nine-member panel
with $1.27$ effective independent opinions. Discussion raises $\varphi$ to $0.990$, leaving
$1.01$. Self-consistency supplies $97\%$ of the $10.7$\,pp of headroom, while specialty role
prompts add $+0.37$\,pp to the oracle ceiling. Computed from raw $\varphi$, mixing model
generations within one vendor yields $2.60$ effective opinions out of nine, and mixing models
from six vendors yields $2.29$.

Decentralized recovers $17.7\%$ of headroom and Centralized $14.8\%$. Independent recovers
$4.9\%$, and Hybrid has $\kappa = -7.4\%$. Neither learned selector improves on plurality.

Discussion raises unanimity to $98.0\%$ while $44.9\%$ of unanimous answers are wrong
(Decentralized, $N{=}9$; Table~\ref{tab:safety}).

Before deploying a panel, compare it with the single doctor and budget-matched self-consistency
on the intended case mix.

Making medical panels agree is easy; making them verifiable is the open problem.
