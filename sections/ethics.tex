\section*{Ethics Statement}

This work uses only public benchmark items derived from licensing examinations and published case
collections. No patient data, no human subjects and no clinical deployment are involved.

The findings identify a deployment risk: panels present shared errors as expert consensus
(\S\ref{sec:safety}). Apparent expert consensus also poses a risk of medical misinformation
that reads as authoritative.

Our findings motivate three mitigations. First, do not present unanimity as a trust cue in a
clinical interface. Reporting ``all five specialists agree'' conveys agreement that requires
separate verification. Second, show the panel's \emph{disagreements} without resolving them into
a single consensus. Third, route verification to retrieved evidence or a clinician.
\citet{zoller2025hybrid} attribute gains in human--model collective diagnosis to complementary
errors.

We report the failure mode as a measured property of systems already being built and deployed,
not as a novel capability.
