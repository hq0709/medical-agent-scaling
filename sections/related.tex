\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Why pooled judgements help, and what they require.}
Collective-intelligence results in medicine rest on error independence: pooling independent
diagnostic judgements improves accuracy~\citep{kurvers2016boosting, wolf2015collective},
plateauing at roughly five raters~\citep{kammer2017}, and groups of nine outperform individuals on
the Human Dx platform~\citep{barnett2019}, the clinical instance of a general
principle~\citep{surowiecki2004wisdom} that multidisciplinary tumour boards
institutionalise~\citep{radcliffe2019tumorboard}. The framing question is not whether two heads
beat one but when~\citep{hautz2024}. The same literature documents what happens when independence
fails: conformity to a unanimous majority~\citep{asch1956conformity} and groupthink in
deliberating groups~\citep{janis1972groupthink}. Correlated errors across models bound ensemble
benefit in general~\citep{correlated2025}, and multi-agent debate can converge on
confident-but-wrong answers~\citep{demystifying_mad}. This paper measures that failure directly in
clinical panels, where diagnostic error is already common enough in ordinary
practice~\citep{graber2013diagnosticerror, singh2014frequency} that manufactured agreement is a
safety concern rather than an efficiency one. The strongest evidence that the independence premise
is what matters comes from the hybrid case: pooling $40{,}762$ physician differentials with five
frontier models over $2{,}133$ open-ended vignettes, \citet{zoller2025hybrid} find that
human--model collectives beat physician collectives \emph{and} model ensembles, and attribute the
margin to humans and models making different kinds of errors. Their result is the consequence; the
quantity that produces it is the one we measure. It also establishes the regime: independence
arrives when the added judgement comes from outside the model ecosystem, which is the boundary our
diversity ladder (\S\ref{sec:premise}) locates from the inside.

\paragraph{Medical language models and multi-agent systems.}
Medical question answering became a capability benchmark once models matched licensing-exam
thresholds~\citep{kung2023usmle, nori2023capabilities, singhal2023medpalm, singhal2025medpalm2},
and later work moved to conducting encounters~\citep{tu2025amie, mcduff2025amie}. Prompting
strategy carries much of that progress~\citep{nori2024medprompt}, which is why architectural
claims need budget-matched controls. On the multi-agent side, MedAgents~\citep{tang2024medagents}
introduced role-played specialist collaboration, MDAgents~\citep{kim2024mdagents} added adaptive
routing, and MAC~\citep{chen2025mac} and TeamMedAgents~\citep{teammedagents} formalised teamwork
components, the latter sweeping team sizes 2--5. Every published medical ablation stops at
$N \leq 5$, none reports a dose--response curve, and none runs a budget-matched single-model
control. Skeptical evaluations find that orchestration often fails to beat well-prompted single
models at substantially higher cost~\citep{medagentboard, npjdm2026, confagents}; our independence
measurement supplies the mechanism those results lack. The pattern has since diversified along
every axis except the one measured here. \citet{chen2024rareagents} and \citet{zhou2025mam}
specialise the roster---an MDT for rare disease, role-assigned radiologist and generalist agents
for multimodal input---and \citet{liu2025medchat} adds a director to coordinate them.
\citet{ma2025medla} replaces majority voting with logical contradiction resolution and
\citet{li2025macd} lets agents accumulate diagnostic knowledge across a cohort, both of which are
attempts to escape a voting rule rather than to escape the correlation that makes voting weak.
\citet{fan2024aihospital}, \citet{sanghvi2026medxagent} and \citet{sviridov2025threemdbench}
move the setting itself, from single-shot selection to simulated, interactive and multimodal
encounters where agents must ask before they answer, and \citet{chen2026map} and
\citet{shi2026phoagents} carry the pattern into inpatient pathways and outcome prediction, where
triage, diagnosis and treatment are separate coordinated stages. These are complementary: each varies the aggregation rule, the roster or the interaction
protocol, and each is a candidate for raising the capture rate we define in \S\ref{sec:ceiling}.
None of them measures the error correlation among its own agents, so none can say whether the
signal its mechanism is competing for is present.

Closest to our central question, \citet{yuan2026mixedvendor} compare single-vendor and
mixed-vendor panels of three frontier models on rare-disease diagnosis and report that mixed-vendor
teams win, attributing the gain to complementary inductive biases surfaced by an overlap analysis.
That is the same lever we isolate in Table~\ref{tab:diversity}, measured with a different
instrument and in the regime our results predict is the favourable one---three models of
comparable strength, where decorrelation carries no quality penalty. Two of our findings bear on
how such comparisons should be read. Raw overlap and raw correlation are depressed mechanically
when members differ in accuracy (Eq.~\ref{eq:phimax}), so vendor-diversity effects should be
reported normalised; and the lever is small wherever we can measure it, moving a nine-member
panel from $1.31$ to $2.59$ effective opinions within one vendor and to $2.55$---not further---once
the panel spans two.

\paragraph{Scaling agents, and scaling inference.}
Debate protocols improve factuality~\citep{du2024debate, liang2024encouraging} and frameworks such
as MetaGPT~\citep{hong2024metagpt} and AutoGen~\citep{wu2024autogen} made the pattern easy to
deploy~\citep{zhang2024exploring}. On how far it scales, ``more agents is all you
need''~\citep{li2024more} reported monotone gains, while \citet{chen2024morecalls} showed the
relationship is non-monotone and \citet{mas_scaling2606} confirmed an inverted-U in agent count;
\citet{diversity2602} attributed the shape to opinion diversity, showing two diverse agents can
match sixteen homogeneous ones. \citet{kim2026scaling} fit a predictive scaling principle across
architectures, model families and agentic benchmarks, and supply the architecture taxonomy and
controlled-comparison design our grid is built on. Test-time compute offers the alternative to
adding agents~\citep{kaplan2020scaling, hoffmann2022chinchilla, brown2024monkeys,
snell2024scaling}, and self-consistency~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} over
chain-of-thought~\citep{wei2022cot} is the budget-matched control we run at every cell.

\paragraph{Confidence and calibration.}
Our safety analysis depends on whether stated confidence is informative. Models are partially
aware of what they know~\citep{kadavath2022know}, but elicited confidence is weakly calibrated and
sensitive to how it is asked~\citep{xiong2024can, guo2024calibration}, and clinical evaluations
report fluent but unreliable responses~\citep{goodman2024accuracy} with demographic biases that
propagate~\citep{omiye2023bias, zack2024bias}. We add a coordination-specific finding: deliberation
degrades the discriminative power of confidence rather than improving it.
