\section{Measuring what a consultation adds}

\subsection{The measurement problem}
\label{sec:metrics}

Complementary and redundant panels produce the same
observable: several agents, an aggregation step, one answer. Accuracy cannot separate them.
A panel can gain a point because its members caught each other's errors, or because nine
correlated draws from one distribution landed on the mode more often than one draw did.
This is why the multi-agent medical
literature can report gains and the sceptical literature can report that orchestration fails to
beat a well-prompted single model~\citep{medagentboard, npjdm2026, confagents} without either
side being wrong.

The premise the design pattern inherits is specific enough to test. Pooling judgements improves
accuracy when the judgements are independent; correlated judgements are merely repeated. So we
instrument each episode with the two quantities that premise depends on, and report them
alongside accuracy throughout.

\paragraph{How many opinions a panel contains.}
Writing $\varphi$ for the mean pairwise error correlation between panel positions (the
correlation of their per-item error indicators), the effective number of independent opinions in a
panel of $N$ agents, with $n_a \equiv N$, is
\begin{equation}
N_{\mathrm{eff}} = \frac{N}{1 + (N-1)\varphi}.
\label{eq:neff}
\end{equation}
At $\varphi = 0$ the $N$ opinions are independent and $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = N$; at $\varphi = 1$
they are one opinion copied $N$ times and $N_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1$. This is the
design-effect correction for clustered observations. Applied to a panel of agents, it converts
nominal panel size into the number of effective independent opinions.

Raw $\varphi$ depends on the members' error rates. Two binary error
indicators with unequal marginals cannot reach $\varphi = 1$: with error rates $p \le q$ the
attainable maximum is
\begin{equation}
\varphi_{\max} = \sqrt{\tfrac{p\,(1-q)}{q\,(1-p)}},
\label{eq:phimax}
\end{equation}
which falls as the members' accuracies diverge. A panel assembled from models of different strength therefore posts a lower
raw $\varphi$ whether or not its members reason differently. We report $\varphi/\varphi_{\max}$
wherever members differ in accuracy.


\paragraph{What a panel holds, and what its aggregation keeps.}
Independence sets headroom; an architecture determines panel accuracy. Let the
\emph{oracle ceiling} $P_{\text{oracle}}$ be the accuracy of a rule that selects a correct
first-round opinion whenever any member holds one. It bounds aggregation rules that select from
those opinions without seeing the label. Collaboration gain then factors exactly:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{P - \PSA}_{\text{gain}}
\;=\;
\underbrace{(P_{\text{oracle}} - \PSA)}_{\text{headroom}}
\;\times\;
\underbrace{\kappa}_{\text{capture}},
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
where $\PSA$ is the single-doctor baseline on the same items and the capture rate $\kappa$ is the
fraction of headroom an architecture recovers. Here $\kappa = 0$ is the single-doctor baseline
and $\kappa = 100\%$ is the oracle. A negative $\kappa$ means panel accuracy is below the
single-doctor baseline. The factorisation is definitional. The two factors have different causes
and remedies. Headroom depends on independent opinions; $\kappa$ depends on selecting a correct
opinion among members who disagree.

We also record \emph{error amplification} $\Ae$: how often the panel held the correct option among
its first opinions and output a wrong one, normalised by the marginal probability that a
single agent is wrong. This differs from \citet{kim2026scaling}'s
$A_e = E_{\text{MAS}}/E_{\text{SAS}}$.

\subsection{Conditions}

\begin{figure*}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig0_architectures.pdf}
\caption{\textbf{The coordination structures we evaluate} are shown with the clinical role each instantiates. \textsc{Self-consistency} and \textsc{Generic panel} vary no topology and therefore do not appear.}
\label{fig:arch}
\end{figure*}

An agent system is a triple $(A, C, \Omega)$: agents, a communication graph, and an aggregation
rule (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). The taxonomy is the one used for general-domain agent
systems~\citep{kim2026scaling}; the clinical instantiations are ours. All conditions share
the item rendering and answer schema. Specialist panels share specialty role prompts;
\textsc{Self-consistency} and \textsc{Generic panel} use the generic prompt. Panel members and
self-consistency samples run at temperature $0.7$, while single-doctor baselines and
orchestrator/triage turns run at $0.3$. Reasoning models ignore temperature.

\paragraph{\textsc{Single}.} One agent answers, zero-shot or with chain-of-thought. It is the baseline
$\PSA$ against which every gain in this paper is measured, run on the same items in the same
cell as the panels it is compared to.

\paragraph{\textsc{Self-consistency}.} The same model, the same \emph{generic} prompt, $k$ samples
at the panel-member temperature, aggregated by majority vote at a matched dollar budget.
Comparing it to a specialist panel measures what specialty role prompts contribute over
sampling the same model repeatedly.

\paragraph{\textsc{Independent}.} $N$ specialists receive the item and answer without seeing one
another; aggregation is majority vote, ties broken by mean stated confidence and then option
order. With $C = \emptyset$, specialty role prompts and sampling produce disagreement.
This condition measures role prompting without communication.

\paragraph{\textsc{Centralized}.} An orchestrator receives the $N$ opinions on a star topology
and must \emph{audit before aggregating}: name the single opinion most likely to be wrong and
justify that, then either commit or re-task one named specialty, which re-answers with the
critique in hand. Specialists never see each other, so the star is preserved. The orchestrator
plays the attending physician, a designated point at which an error can be caught before it
reaches the output. This condition tests whether an orchestrator improves $\kappa$.

\paragraph{\textsc{Decentralized}.} $N$ specialists give first opinions, then exchange them on a
complete graph for up to three rounds, each free to revise. Rounds stop early only on true
unanimity, defined as every agent producing the same \emph{valid} option; a panel containing an
unparsed answer is not unanimous. This condition models a multidisciplinary team meeting and
measures independence and agreement before and after discussion.

\paragraph{\textsc{Hybrid}.} A generalist answers first with a stated confidence; the triage
gate escalates the case to the $N$-specialist panel when that confidence falls below a frozen
threshold $\theta$. A panel with no majority escalates to full discussion. $\theta$ is calibrated
once on a 100-item development slice disjoint from every evaluation set. This is confidence-gated
escalation from one generalist to specialists, distinct from the Hybrid topology of
\citet{kim2026scaling} (an orchestrator plus limited peer edges).

\paragraph{\textsc{Generic panel}.} \textsc{Independent}, \textsc{Centralized} and
\textsc{Decentralized} rebuilt out of undifferentiated internists: every member receives the same
generic system prompt. The model, temperature, architecture and items match the specialist
conditions, so the comparison isolates the contribution of specialty role prompts.

\paragraph{Panel composition and nesting.}
\label{sec:nested} A cheap router (\texttt{gpt-4.1-nano}, temperature 0)
ranks the nine most relevant specialties for each item from a fixed 17-specialty list; a panel
of size $N$ is the first $N$ of that ranking. Every agent's first opinion is keyed on
$(\text{item}, \text{specialty}_i, \text{seed}, i)$ and never on $N$ or on the architecture, so
panels are \emph{nested} (the $N{=}9$ panel contains the $N{=}5$ panel), and all four
multi-agent conditions reuse the same first opinions. The nesting is a common-random-numbers
coupling that fixes the estimand as \emph{accuracy as one routed panel is extended} rather than
the expected accuracy of independently redrawn panels.

\subsection{Clinical tasks}

Three benchmarks, 250 items each, sampled with a fixed seed:
MedXpertQA-Text~\citep{zuo2025medxpertqa} (10 options; stratified by the 12 body systems $\times$
3 task types), MedQA-USMLE~\citep{jin2021medqa} (4 options), and the contamination-screened hard
split of MedAgentsBench~\citep{medagentsbench} (894 items excluding the
\texttt{medqa\_5options} subset; the first 250 after stratified shuffling). The endpoint is exact
match against the dataset's own gold key. No LLM judge appears anywhere in the evaluation path.

Agent systems on
general-domain benchmarks call tools, and tool-call overhead dominates the token accounting and
confounds any comparison of coordination structures; a panel that coordinates more also retrieves
more, and the two effects are not separable from the output. Here no condition calls a tool, so
no tool-call overhead enters the difference between an architecture and the single-doctor
baseline on the same item.
